# Competitive Landscape: German Trades Software

Status: living — dated research snapshot (2026-07-23); refresh volatile figures before using them in a current decision

This document is a research reference for WerkFlow's competitive environment in German trade-business software (`Handwerkersoftware`). It focuses on software used by HVAC / SHK, plumbing, roofing, tiling, electrical, and adjacent construction trades.

It is intended to help future product and engineering work:

- understand what established and newer products already cover;
- distinguish table-stakes capabilities from meaningful product choices;
- learn from recurring user praise and complaints;
- compare packaging and pricing without relying on sales-page memory;
- identify questions worth validating with real SHK businesses.

It is **not** a purchasing guide, a feature roadmap, or proof that a competitor's advertised capability works well in practice.

> **Research snapshot:** 23 July 2026  
> **Market:** Germany first, with DACH context where a vendor operates across the region  
> **Evidence boundary:** public web sources only; no paid competitor account or sales demo was used

## How To Read This Document

Three evidence types are kept separate:

- **Vendor fact:** a capability, price, customer count, or company detail stated by the vendor. This proves what the vendor publicly claims, not independently verified product quality.
- **Review signal:** an experience reported by a reviewer or summarized by a review platform. It is subjective and may not represent the full customer base.
- **WerkFlow analysis:** an inference, product question, or possible lesson derived from the evidence. It is not a claim about the competitor.

`Not publicly evidenced` means the research did not find reliable public confirmation. It does **not** mean the capability is absent. Likewise, a checkmark in a comparison table means public evidence exists, not that the feature is complete, included in every plan, or easy to use.

Pricing and review scores are volatile. Always use the dated snapshot and follow the linked source before relying on a number in a current decision.

## Scope And Selection Method

There is no reliable public league table for the largest German Handwerkersoftware companies by revenue, active businesses, or paid seats. The vendors are private, publish different metrics, and use terms such as `Kunden`, `Betriebe`, `Handwerker`, `Nutzer`, and app downloads inconsistently. These figures must not be normalized into a fabricated ranking.

The six full profiles were selected using a combination of:

1. direct relevance to SHK and at least one of roofing or tiling;
2. breadth across office and field workflows;
3. German/DACH market presence;
4. a public scale signal or unusually strong review footprint;
5. strategic relevance to WerkFlow's product direction;
6. representation of both modern cloud products and established full-suite ERP incumbents.

The result is a **scale-supported, strategically representative set**, not a definitive rank from first to sixth.

## Research Method

### Source Priority

Sources were used in this order:

1. official pricing, product, help-center, terms, security, and company pages;
2. Apple App Store and Google Play product pages;
3. direct review-platform profiles, especially Trustpilot, OMR Reviews, and ProvenExpert;
4. reputable trade publications and neutral market overviews;
5. secondary comparison sites only for discovery or when clearly labeled.

Vendor-authored competitor comparisons were not treated as neutral evidence. Search-result snippets were not used to fill a gap when the underlying page could not be inspected.

### Review Method

For each competitor, the research:

- records the platform, score, visible review count, and access date;
- samples recent positive and critical reviews rather than relying only on the aggregate;
- treats an issue as recurring only when multiple reviews independently describe it;
- separates reviews of a mobile app from reviews of the full software and commercial relationship;
- records when a company invites reviews or when a profile aggregates other sources;
- avoids turning a review allegation into an established fact.

Review scores are not directly comparable across platforms. OMR verifies B2B reviewer identity and conflicts under its own process; Trustpilot allows open submissions and may show invited or verified reviews; app stores disproportionately measure mobile-app experience; ProvenExpert can aggregate external sources. Small samples are especially fragile.

Review-method sources: [OMR review verification process](https://hi.omr.com/en/success-hub/omr-reviews-review-process), [Trustpilot's explanation on company profile pages](https://de.trustpilot.com/review/hero-software.de), and [ProvenExpert verification information](https://info.provenexpert.com/de/massnahmen-zur-verifizierung). Accessed 23 July 2026.

### Example Team Cost: A Comparison Aid, Not A Quote

For a hypothetical ten-person business with two office users and eight field users, a rough formula is more honest than comparing headline prices:

- HERO: one base office license + one additional standard license + eight app licenses; the required plan depends on feature needs and company size.
- ToolTime: Max includes up to ten employees, subject to the active annual/monthly offer and add-ons.
- plancraft: a tier with the required functions + any office seats beyond those included + eight mobile seats.
- Craftnote: two `Büro` or `Büro Plus` seats + eight `Baustelle` seats.
- STREIT: quote for office licenses, field apps, deployment model, migration, and training.
- pds: two office workplaces at the applicable suite range + eight app licenses + any implementation or add-ins.

Do not turn these formulas into a decision without fresh quotes and a feature-normalized scenario. Cheap field licenses can matter more than the base price in a trade business, while implementation and migration can dominate first-year cost for an established suite.

#### Evidence-Backed Strengths

- The most complete public inventory story among the modern cloud competitors: stock, movements, reservations, packing, consumption, suppliers, orders, and scanning.
- Broad job-to-cash chain and close integration between office, field, documents, time, and payments.
- Clear role-based license distinction between office and field users.
- Active investment in narrow AI intake plus wider deterministic workflow automation.
- Large app-store footprint and current vendor scale/funding.

#### Evidence-Backed Risks And Open Questions

- Public pricing requires careful normalization across annual, flexible, promotional, app, and implementation charges.
- Current review evidence raises real questions about onboarding capacity, migration promises, support entitlements, and contract flexibility.
- Generic “offline” language should be decomposed by workflow.
- The DMS's organization-wide navigation and role model are not sufficiently visible publicly.
- Copilot's deterministic versus generative parts, API objects, AI limits, and Plus implementation scope need a demo and written terms.
- The product may be over-scoped for very small businesses despite Select positioning.

#### WerkFlow Lessons

- Connect planned materials, reservations, physical movements, job use, billability, and supplier orders without collapsing them into one ambiguous “material” state.
- Treat implementation promises, support channels, and migration acceptance criteria as part of the product.
- Keep field/offline claims workflow-specific and show last-sync/failure state.
- Separate currently available AI from announced modules.
- Decide deliberately which capabilities are core, role-gated, usage-metered, or premium.
- Preserve WerkFlow's simple employee experience even as office functionality expands.

### ToolTime

#### Positioning And Company

ToolTime GmbH is based in Berlin. The company says it has developed ToolTime since 2019; a 2023 funding announcement dates the company founding to 2018. ToolTime is positioned as a cloud all-in-one for offers/invoices, planning, time, site documentation, and payments, primarily for small and medium trade businesses across SHK, electrical, roofing, construction, painting, joinery, cleaning, and landscaping.

The current company page claims more than 20,000 customers and more than 80 employees. ## Cross-Competitor Findings For WerkFlow

The following are product analyses derived from the sourced profiles, not additional competitor facts.

### 1. The Market Is Split Between Modern Usability And Incumbent Depth

HERO, ToolTime, plancraft, and Craftnote generally present faster-starting cloud experiences. STREIT and pds show how deep an SHK operating system can become after decades of commercial, service, inventory, accounting, and interface development. WerkFlow's opportunity is to combine role-specific simplicity with a deliberately chosen subset of that depth. Attempting incumbent breadth without progressive disclosure would undermine the product's target users.

### 2. “Materials” Can Mean Five Different Products

Public competitor pages use similar language for materially different capability:

1. a price/catalog item used in a quote;
2. planned material reserved for a job;
3. physical stock by warehouse or location;
4. procurement from supplier through receipt;
5. consumed material that flows into post-calculation and customer billing.

WerkFlow should keep these concepts distinct in code, UI, docs, and future packaging. HERO, STREIT, and pds are the strongest public references in this set for multi-step stock/procurement flows. ToolTime, plancraft, and Craftnote should not be credited with full warehouse control merely because they expose materials.

### 3. Onboarding, Support, And Contract Clarity Are Product Features

Reviews do not only judge buttons. They repeatedly discuss migration, training, support access, internal key-user effort, contract terms, and pricing surprises. A simpler product can still fail if the business cannot migrate its customer, article, document, and open-job data safely.

WerkFlow should eventually document:

- what can be imported by the customer;
- what assisted migration includes;
- who owns data validation;
- expected onboarding time by company size;
- support channels and response expectations;
- cancellation, export, retention, and deletion behavior.

### 4. One Employee App Is A Strategic Differentiator

The incumbent products use several specialized apps. That can create deep task-specific behavior, but also licensing and navigation complexity. Craftnote demonstrates the adoption advantage of field-first simplicity. WerkFlow's future React Native product should prefer one shell with role- and assignment-aware surfaces for jobs, time, absence, documents, photos, inventory, and communication.

Offline support should be defined per workflow, not marketed as a binary product property. For every offline-capable action, define available data, mutation queueing, attachment behavior, conflict resolution, user-visible sync state, and recovery.

### 5. German Trade Standards Are Table Stakes In Commercial Depth

DATANORM, IDS, UGL, Open Masterdata, GAEB, DATEV, ZUGFeRD, XRechnung, Peppol, REB/VOB, and §13b recur across mature products. WerkFlow does not need to implement all of them immediately, but commercial and inventory architecture should avoid making them impossible. Each future module should state which standard, version, direction, and workflow it supports rather than saying only “integration available.”

### 6. Documents Are Both Context And Governance

Competitors commonly attach documents, photos, reports, and signatures to a project or job. Mature suites also emphasize automatic archive, retention, and accounting records. WerkFlow's combination of contextual document access and a central manager library is a useful foundation. The next comparison questions are bulk export, legal retention, search across entities, version history, audit trails, offline packs, and recovery.

### 7. AI Is Becoming Workflow-Specific

Current public examples include phone intake, report rewriting, translation, delivery-note extraction/matching, invoice reading, and workflow automation. The useful pattern is not generic text generation: it is turning an operational input into a structured, reviewable artifact. Future WerkFlow AI work should always show the source, proposed change, human approval point, audit record, organization boundary, retention behavior, and fallback when confidence is low.

### 8. Review Scores Are Discovery Tools, Not Product Truth

The strongest lessons come from repeated, concrete themes across recent reviews, not the average star value. Invitation practices, tiny samples, outdated app versions, aggregated profiles, and region-specific businesses can all distort the headline. Future competitive work should preserve this document's platform/count/date/caveat format.

## Recommended Demo And Customer-Interview Test Script

Use one normalized scenario across products and WerkFlow. This is more useful than asking whether a feature exists.

1. Import a customer and article catalog, including awkward SHK fields and supplier prices.
2. Receive a customer request, create a job, assign an employee, and reschedule it.
3. Prepare an offer, convert it through execution to a compliant partial/final invoice, and correct a mistake.
4. Work the assignment offline: navigate, clock travel/work/break time, take photos, complete a form, capture material, and collect a signature.
5. Restore connectivity with conflicting edits and a large attachment queue.
6. Reserve material, order a shortage, receive it, move stock, consume it on the job, return unused stock, and make consumed material billable.
7. Find every customer/job document from both context and a central archive; inspect version and audit history.
8. Correct time or material with the intended approval and audit trail.
9. Export the organization's customers, documents, jobs, time, and stock in a usable form.
10. Price the same two-office/eight-field-user setup for year one and year three, including onboarding, migration, training, support, apps, storage, and termination.

## Maintenance And Refresh Checklist

This document is a dated research artifact. Refresh it when a major WerkFlow roadmap decision depends on it, when a competitor launches a material module, or at least every six months while the market is actively changing.

For every refresh:

- preserve the access date for every changed price, rating, review count, and scale claim;
- re-open the underlying page rather than trusting a search result or this snapshot;
- record conflicting vendor figures instead of silently selecting the most favorable one;
- distinguish regular price, temporary promotion, annual-equivalent price, and quote-only costs;
- sample recent positive and critical reviews and record invitation/aggregation caveats;
- separate app-store experience from the whole software and commercial relationship;
- change `not publicly evidenced` only when a reliable public source or a documented hands-on test supports it;
- add hands-on demo findings in a separate dated section so they are not confused with public research;
- update the WerkFlow baseline from the feature docs before changing comparison conclusions;
- check whether new findings imply a product-documentation update, not only a change here.
