## Read This First

This record is the plan of the next slice. It grew in two steps: on 2026-09-15 pre-Wave-3 step 3 placed the `Plantafel` here as the week view's successor; on 2026-09-18, after the production release, the owner extended the slice to every calendar view, because the calendar is the office's main hub and its current state ("cropped corners, a stray scrollbar, an off-centre now dot, thick low-contrast lines, sluggish non-optimistic drags, generic error messages") is not acceptable for the product's most-used surface. The [2026-09-18 audit](#audit-of-2026-09-18) below is the verified evidence for that judgement.

Two rules for the agent that takes this slice:

- This plan is a floor, not a ceiling. It names the defects that are known and the direction that is decided; it does not claim to list every improvement the calendar needs. Run your own discovery on the code and in the browser at every viewport, research the references named below with your own eyes, and propose what this plan missed in the pre-implementation report. A local maximum of "the old views with thinner lines" fails this slice.
- Bold changes are wanted, safeguards are not negotiable. The range owner's freshness contract, the mutation ownership, the authorization of every action, the qualification, capacity, supersession, commitment and series dialogs, and the catalog clauses that describe today's behaviour all stay. What changes is the rendering, the interaction engine, the visual language, the optimistic behaviour of every drop, and the words the user reads when something is refused.

Before changing anything: load the `werkflow-design` skill, read the [calendar spec](../../../features/calendar-and-resource-planning.md) including its open decisions, the [freshness contract](../../../technical/realtime-and-caching.md), the [step 3 record](../pre-wave-3/03-clock-fab-and-calendar-ux.md#the-calendar-and-the-plantafel) (the board discovery and the market table), and the P1-11, P1-12, P1-14, P1-21 and P1-22 slice records under this folder.

## Bounded Outcome

The calendar becomes one coherent, fast planning surface built on one rendering and interaction layer:

- The week view becomes the `Plantafel`: a people-row board over one to six weeks with multi-day bars, absences, holidays, closure days and capacity in the row, dispatch state on every card, drag-and-drop dispatch between rows and days with keyboard and form alternatives, the Parkplatz as a drag source, notes, a read-only mode and per-user persistence. It stores nothing new and calls only existing actions. Employees see the same component with their own single row.
- The day view and the month view are rebuilt on the same layer: one grid language (hairlines from tokens, no bordered cell cards, no clipped corners, no stray scrollbars, one now indicator), one drag engine that moves a card under the pointer without a React commit per frame, and drops that are optimistic in every view under the range owner's mutation ownership, with Undo after persistence and rollback with a specific message on failure.
- Every calendar action that can be refused tells the user which rule refused it and what to do next, in German, at the point of action; no generic "konnte nicht" message and no raw error code survives.
- The calendar is measured at interaction level (drag, drop, resize, view switch) and holds reviewed budgets on the typical profile, at 1280×720 and 2560×1440 alike.

The owner decided on 2026-09-15 that the board replaces the week view rather than sitting beside it, and on 2026-09-18 that the day and month views and every calendar message belong to the same slice; the discovery and the market evidence are in [pre-Wave-3 step 3](../pre-wave-3/03-clock-fab-and-calendar-ux.md#the-calendar-and-the-plantafel) and in the [research](#research-of-2026-09-18) below.

## Primary User And Roles

- `admin` and `buero` plan on the board and in the day view: they see every active employee, drag, resize, park, unpark, reassign, add notes and switch the read-only mode. The month view is their overview of absences, holidays, closures and load per day.
- `employee` sees the board as "Woche" with exactly their own row; no drag, no other people, no capacity of others. The day and month views show only their own occurrences and time, as today.
- Outsiders and members of another organization see nothing of the calendar's data.

## Verified Current Baseline

### Verified on 2026-09-15 in the code

- `components/kalender/week-view/week-view.tsx` (893 lines) is a people-row day board: one row per member, seven day columns, a `min-w-[1150px]` grid with a sticky name column, up to three time blocks and three job badges per cell with "+n mehr", unassigned jobs in the day header, drag of job badges between rows and days and from the Parkplatz, drag of closed time sessions, a cell click that opens the member-day dialog. Managers see the members selected in the filter; employees see only themselves.
- The week view receives no absence, holiday, closure or capacity data. Absences render in the month view only. Capacity exists per planning action (`CapacityEvaluation` in `lib/planning/types.ts`), not per displayed window.
- `components/kalender/use-calendar-range-data.ts` and `GET /api/calendar-window` already return occurrences, time entries, vacation, sickness and holiday context for a window. `PlanningCalendarEntry` carries no dispatch state; the Einsätze panel reads it through `getDispatchOverview`.
- Only the month view uses FullCalendar (`calendar-container.tsx:1609`); the day and week views are hand-rolled and share the container's range owner, mutation ownership and drag state.

### Contradicted or sharpened

- The audit says period close is not checked on `updateEntry` and `reassignEntries`. Confirmed: neither action, nor `reassignEntryBatch`, consults `time_periods`/closed periods (`grep period_closed lib/time-tracking/actions.ts` finds nothing). A manager can drag a time block inside a closed period. Recorded as finding **F-1 for the time-tracking owner** (`P1-23` semantics, not this slice's to change): the calendar will show the server's answer, and the server has no answer yet. Listed in the roadmap's Wave 3 planning as a `P1-33`/time follow-up and in the time-tracking spec's limitations.
- The action result types the message map must cover are `error: string` in every action the calendar calls (`UpdateJobResult`, `PlanningActionResult`, `WorkActionFailure`, `UpdateEntryResult`, `RequestChangeResult`, the reassign results). A union "built from the action result types" is therefore not derivable by `tsc` today. The map is exhaustive over a hand-listed union (`satisfies Record<CalendarActionErrorCode, …>`, tier 1 for the map's own completeness) and a unit test scans the calendar-reachable action modules for `error: '<code>'` literals and fails on any code the union does not carry (tier 2), which is the strongest guarantee reachable without narrowing five domains' result types in a calendar slice. Narrowing those types is a backlog row.
- `next dev` (Next 16 `agentRules`) appends a ten-line block to the repository's `AGENTS.md` on every start. Reverted; `agentRules: false` in `next.config.ts` lands with package A (harness hygiene, no product effect).
- Colour literals outside the calendar: five `hsl(` strings in `components/password/PasswordStrengthMeter.tsx`. The new lint selector covers all product JSX, so the meter moves to the semantic tokens in the same change (four steps map to destructive, warning, success-text, success).

### Added (what the plan left vague, decided here)

1. **The board read is a sixth dataset of the range owner, not a side channel.** `GET /api/calendar-board` returns, for the same window as the calendar GET, the rows (active employee records with team, login state and user id), per person-day capacity (target, absence, planned, overbooked) and non-working flags, per (occurrence, employee record) dispatch state, and per job the material readiness dimension from `composeReadiness`. `readWindow` fires both GETs in parallel and commits both under one generation, so freshness, mutation ownership, retained-grid and stale states hold for the board data exactly as for the five existing datasets. Every view requires every dataset (the day view shades working time and shows dispatch chips too); the previous day/week exception for absence datasets bought nothing because one GET already fails as a whole.
2. **Capacity is computed by the planning owner, not by the board.** The per-employee-day target resolution inside `assessPlanningOccurrences` is extracted into a shared loader in `lib/planning/server.ts` and reused by the window read, so a board cell and a planning warning can never disagree. Fingerprints stay byte-identical because the extraction moves code without changing values.
3. **Dispatch state per card** comes from the same `loadDispatchViews` derivation the Einsätze panel uses (exported for occurrence sets); a card in a person's row shows that person's recipient state, or „nicht gesendet".
4. **Unpark is one server action** (`unparkJobIntoSchedule` in `lib/planning/actions.ts`): unpark, then the schedule write, then re-park with the previous context if the write fails. A qualification warning returns after the unpark without re-parking, exactly as today's client flow leaves the job while the dialog is open; the client retries the schedule write with the approval or restores the parking on decline.
5. **`revalidatePath('/kalender')` leaves all ten call sites** (planning, dispatch, work lifecycle, time tracking, commitments, parking, maintenance, responsibilities, time corrections). The page is dynamic and the range owner plus Realtime own the calendar's freshness; the members, settings and profile route refresh stays.
6. **Preferences** live under a `calendar` key of `organization_user_preferences.preferences`, written by one new Server Action (`saveCalendarPreferences`) and read by the page through the existing cached preference reader; parsed by zod, invalid JSON falls back to defaults. Remembered: view, horizon, density, weekends, read-only, actual time, filters, search.
7. **Server prefetch follows the landing view**: the page prefetches the window of the view the user lands on (board for managers, day for employees, or the remembered view) including absences, holidays and the board context, so the first paint is covered for every dataset and the client reads again only on a scope change.
8. **Board layout** is one CSS grid per row: `grid-template-columns` from the horizon, cards placed by a pure lane packer (`lib/calendar/board-layout.ts`) with `grid-column` spans for multi-day bars, so row height is content-driven for free and absences are bars in the same lanes. Longer horizons scroll sideways inside the region with a sticky name column; one week fits at 1280 px (Q5).
9. **Month cells** grow with the grid (`grid-template-rows: repeat(6, minmax(0, 1fr))`); each cell renders as many cards as its measured height allows and one „+n mehr" button that opens the day list in a `Popover`. Nothing hidden is mounted. Absences, holidays and closures are per-day bar segments with joined edges; the same segment component draws multi-day bars on the board.
10. **Employee board on a phone**: the own row renders as a day list (one section per date with the person's cards), never the grid.
11. **The AGENTS.md dev-server edit** is a harness defect fixed in `next.config.ts`, recorded as tier 1 (the option removes the write).

### The drag engine's contract (`components/kalender/drag-engine/`)

- `useDragEngine({ surfaceRef, resolveSlot, onTarget, onDrop, onCancel })` returns `startDrag(event, session)`. A session names the payload (`occurrence`, `parkedJob`, `untimedJob`, `timeBlock`, `barEdge`, `createRange`), the ghost (width, height, label), the origin slot and the pointer offset.
- Pointer capture on the handle, a 5 px threshold before a drag starts, a 250 ms long press on touch, `touch-action: none` on handles. Every `pointermove` is coalesced into one `requestAnimationFrame`; the ghost moves by `transform` on a fixed element; the target highlight is one absolutely positioned element per surface moved by `transform`; auto-scroll near the edges runs inside the same frame loop.
- `resolveSlot(x, y)` reads a slot map the view builds at drag start (row offsets, column offsets, container rect and scroll offsets) and never calls `getBoundingClientRect` per move. Side panels register drop zones with rects captured at drag start.
- `onTarget(slot)` runs the view's pre-check; the result (valid or refused with a message code) is written into the ghost's DOM (class and text) without React state.
- `Escape` cancels, `pointercancel` cancels, a drop calls `onDrop(slot, { copy, fine })` with the modifier state; no React commit happens between start and drop, which the component contract proves with a `Profiler` around the surface.

### State and rollback model per drop kind

| Drop | Optimistic write into the range owner | Server call | Rollback | Undo |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reassign or move a visit (board, day, month) | `updateJobs`: the entry's date, time, duration, assignees | `updateJob` (legacy) or `updatePlanningCalendarEntry` through the existing dialog wrapper | previous entry restored | inverse update |
| Extend an all-day bar | `endDateExclusive`, `estimatedDurationMinutes` | same | same | same |
| Park by drag | entry removed, parked list gains the job, context dialog opens | `parkWorkTarget` from the dialog | cancel or failure restores the entry and removes the parked card | unpark with the previous placement |
| Unpark by drag | parked card removed, entry added | `unparkJobIntoSchedule` (one call; warning path retries the schedule write) | restore both lists; re-park on the server when the schedule write failed | park again |
| Schedule an untimed job | time and assignees | `updateJob` | restore | inverse |
| Move or resize a time block | `updateEntries` timestamps and owner for every source entry | `updateEntry` sequence or `reassignEntryBatch` as today | restore entries | inverse |
| Copy (Alt) | entry added with a temporary id | `createPlanningEntry` | remove the temporary entry | `setPlanningOccurrenceStatus` cancel is not a delete; Undo is not offered for copies (the created entry stays and the banner says so) |

Every operation calls `beginMutation` before the optimistic write and releases in `finally`; the settle read after the release is the truth, so an optimistic state the server refused can survive at most one debounce interval before the read replaces it, and the refusal sentence appears at once from the action result.

### Optimistic UI, Undo and refusal messages

- React 19's `useOptimistic` reverts silently when the action throws; the message is the app's job. TanStack's pattern (snapshot in `onMutate`, roll back in `onError`, refetch in `onSettled`) is what the range owner already does through `beginMutation` and the settle read ([react.dev](https://react.dev/reference/react/useOptimistic), [TanStack](https://tanstack.com/query/latest/docs/framework/react/guides/optimistic-updates)). Mishunov's rule: optimistic only where success is the 97 to 99 % case, and failure must be visible within two seconds ([Smashing](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2016/11/true-lies-of-optimistic-user-interfaces/)). Linear applies the change in memory before saving and rolls back a rejected transaction while keeping previous values for undo; a failure shows a small toast naming the thing that failed (third-party write-up, unverified).
- Google Calendar confirms a drag with a snackbar and Undo and lets `z` undo the last change; Gmail's undo window is 5 to 30 seconds. Raskin: "Never use a warning when you mean undo." Nielsen: confirm only actions with serious consequences, "go to great lengths to provide undo" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog/)).
- Refusal wording that works: Outlook's InfoBar "Konflikte mit einem anderen Termin" and "This appointment occurs in the past"; Google's "You don't have permission to edit this event". NN/g's guidelines: close to the source, human language, precise description, constructive advice, no blame, preserve the input ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/error-message-guidelines/)). GOV.UK: describe what happened and how to fix it; never "please", "sorry", "invalid", "oops", "forbidden" ([GOV.UK](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/error-message/)). Fluent: a toast without an action times out after seven seconds; never use toasts for necessary actions; Atlassian: never auto-dismiss an error flag.

- Rendering techniques: `transform` and `opacity` only for the ghost (web.dev animation guide); `will-change` toggled from script during the drag, never static; `content-visibility: auto` with `contain-intrinsic-size` for off-screen rows (Baseline since 2025-09), with the sticky axes kept outside any contained ancestor because `contain: paint` clips them; `@tanstack/react-virtual` (MIT, 7.6 kB gzip) for boards above roughly fifty rows, which Dynamics' own guidance calls the practical limit per tab; React 19.3's `<ViewTransition>` for view switches under `startTransition`; the React Compiler is stable and Next.js 16 enables it with `reactCompiler: true` and an `annotation` mode for opt-in files, which the implementing agent measures on `components/kalender` before relying on it.

### Principles distilled for this slice

1. Structure is felt, not seen: one hairline token at roughly 8 to 12 % foreground alpha for every grid line, no cell cards, no gutters between cells, no doubled borders (Linear, Toast UI, Google's white-space rule).
2. Today and now are the two loud things in a grid: a subtle today tint of about 5 % plus a strong date marker, and one now indicator drawn identically in every view (Google, Outlook, Toast UI).
3. Text in a card is never below 11 px; absence bars, holiday bars and chips meet 4.5:1 against their fill in both themes (WCAG 1.4.3; the light-theme absence bars fail today).
4. A drop keeps the duration and the time of day; only an explicit edge drag changes length (Dynamics, criterion 22).
5. Snap to 15 minutes by default, 5 minutes with a modifier; the ghost follows the pointer at display refresh through `transform`, never through layout (Bryntum, Mobiscroll, Linear's animation rule).
6. Every move is optimistic, confirmed by a banner with Undo after persistence, reverted with a specific sentence on failure; nothing asks "Wirklich verschieben?" for a reversible move (Raskin, Nielsen, Google).
7. A refusal names the rule and the next step: "Sven Neumann ist am 18.9. im Urlaub. Wähle einen anderen Tag oder eine andere Person." beats "Auftrag konnte nicht verschoben werden." (NN/g, GOV.UK).
8. Density is the user's choice: two modes remembered per user, rows that grow with their content, the day-view zoom ladder (Google 2026, ServiceTitan, Syncfusion, TAIFUN).
9. The board uses the whole screen: at 2560 px the columns and cards grow, at 1280 px seven days still fit without a horizontal scroll, and horizontal scrolling appears only with a longer horizon (Google "responsive to your screen"; Sykasoft's second-monitor habit).
10. Every drag has a non-drag path: a focusable card, Enter for the edit dialog, "Verschieben nach …" and "Zuweisen an …" commands, click-to-drop (WCAG 2.2 2.5.7; Dynamics; ServiceTitan).
11. Colour supplements, never carries: dispatch state and absence kind show an icon or a label beside the tint (Fantastical's dot shapes, the calendar spec's role and UX principles).
12. Overflow is honest: "+n mehr" opens the full day; nothing is mounted hidden (Google, Mobiscroll `maxEventStack`).
13. Weekends are visible and shaded by default and can be hidden per user (HERO, Jobber, Google's "show weekends").
14. Whatever the analog board did in one motion stays one motion: notes in two clicks, a copy by drag with a modifier, a row isolated by clicking the name (Craftboxx, plancraft, Sykasoft).
15. Speed is a feature the user can feel in the first frame: the measured budgets below cover the drag, the drop and the resize, not only the navigation.

## Design Direction

The target that "bold" points at, per surface. The implementing agent renders these in both themes at 1280×720, 1440×900, 1920×1080, 2560×1440, tablet landscape and 375 px before the pre-implementation report and adjusts them with the owner.

### One calendar design language

- Tokens in `app/globals.css`, consumed by every view and panel, none of them hard-coded: `--calendar-grid` (hairline, roughly 8 to 12 % of the foreground), `--calendar-grid-strong` (hour lines and column dividers, about twice that), `--calendar-cell-off` (weekends, holidays, closures, non-working time), `--calendar-today` (about 5 % tint), `--calendar-now` (the indicator), `--calendar-gutter` (the time axis and the name column background), plus the planning, actual-time, absence, holiday and note card families derived from the existing purple, neutral and semantic tokens. A lint rule rejects `rgb(`, `rgba(`, `hsl(` and hex literals in product JSX and in `<style>` strings under `components/kalender` (the existing hex and palette rules miss them), so the calendar cannot regress to literal colours (enforcement ladder tier 2).
- One grid renderer: CSS grid or absolute positioning inside one scroll region per view, grid lines painted by the container (`repeating-linear-gradient` or one border per track), never one DOM node per line; `border-collapse` semantics without a table; the container radius is 8 px (`rounded-lg`) with the header and body inside one clipped region so no border is cut; scroll regions carry `scrollbar-gutter: stable` on the body only, and the header never has a scroller of its own.
- One now indicator: a 2 px line in `--calendar-now` with a 8 px dot centred on the line at the axis edge, drawn once per view from one hook, positioned on whole device pixels (`Math.round` of the pixel offset, no fractional `left` inside a transformed layer), never clipped by a header track, one colour and opacity from top to bottom.
- One card anatomy: title, then customer and place, then a chip row (dispatch state, readiness, series mark, commitment mismatch, provisional time); the same component in the board, the day view and the month view with three size variants; sticky labels inside long bars (Bryntum); `text-xs` minimum.
- One drag engine for every draggable (card, bar edge, time block, Parkplatz card, untimed chip, drag-to-create): pointer events with `setPointerCapture`, a ghost moved by `transform` inside `requestAnimationFrame`, hit testing against a precomputed slot map (no `getBoundingClientRect` per move), no React state during the drag except one throttled hover-target update per frame at most, auto-scroll near the edges, 15-minute snapping, modifier for fine snapping and for copy, touch with a long-press start, Escape cancels, a drop announcement in the live region. Decision Q6 picks the implementation (in-house engine or a library).
- One optimistic owner: every drop writes its intended result into the range owner under `beginMutation` before any server call (the day view's private override maps go away), the card is confirmed with Undo after persistence, rolled back with the specific message on failure, and no action response triggers a page render (`revalidatePath('/kalender')` leaves the planning, dispatch, work-lifecycle and time actions or is scoped so the calendar's own reads own freshness). Unpark becomes one server action. A park drop moves the card first and opens the context dialog second; cancelling the dialog rolls the card back.
- One message layer: `lib/calendar/messages.ts` (name to be confirmed) holds an exhaustive typed map from every calendar action's result code to a German sentence that names the rule and the next step, with placeholders for the person, the date and the time where the code carries them; the type is `Record<CalendarActionErrorCode, …>` over a union built from the action result types, so a new code without a sentence fails `tsc` (tier 1), and a unit test checks that no calendar component renders a raw code (tier 2). Pre-checks that the client can decide (overlap, future time, non-working day, absence, read-only mode, employee without login, internal entries cannot be parked) refuse before the drop with the same sentence, shown as a red preview and an inline message at the pointer, as the overlap preview does today.
- Density: "Kompakt" and "Komfortabel" remembered per user and organization; rows grow with their content (overlap count) from a minimum; the day view keeps its zoom ladder and gains keyboard zoom.
- Dark mode: every token has a dark value; the rendered review covers both themes for every state (today, now, absences, holidays, closures, provisional time, selection, drag ghost, drop target, refused drop).

## Data Ownership And Historical Semantics

The calendar owns no data. Occurrences, assignments, dispatches, absences, schedules, capacity assessments and time projections keep their owners; every calendar action calls the owning action and the owner's history rules apply unchanged. Per-user calendar preferences (horizon, density, weekends, filters, read-only, actual-time toggle) are stored with the existing organization-scoped user preferences.

## Permissions And Organization Isolation

Reads go through the existing window GET (organization-scoped, role-filtered) plus one capacity read with the same authorization. Employees receive only their own occurrences and never another person's capacity or absence. Every drop calls an existing manager-only action that re-authorizes. The capacity read is proven to deny outsiders and to exclude other members' data for employees. An optimistic move never shows an employee a state they are not allowed to see.

## UI And Field-Worker Behavior

Employees keep `Mein Einsatz` and see the board only as their own row with no drag; their day and month views keep today's own-data rule. Managers get the dense board and day view. Every drag has a keyboard and form path. Colours supplement labels and icons and never carry status alone. Touch targets on the field paths stay at 44 px.

## Realtime, Caching, Offline, And Failure Recovery

The calendar consumes the range owner: retained grid, stale and error states, mutation ownership, Undo after persistence and rollback with the error at the point of action apply unchanged and now hold in every view. Another session's change reaches every view within `LIVE_TARGET_MS`. A drop's optimistic state survives a Realtime echo and is replaced only by the settle read. No offline queue. No action response renders the route.

## Migration And Rollback

No schema change. Rollback is the previous rendering (git history); preferences left behind are harmless. If Q2 removes FullCalendar, the five packages leave `package.json` and `bun.lock` in the same change and `knip` proves nothing else imports them.

## Acceptance Criteria

Criteria 1 to 22 were drafted in the step 3 record on 2026-09-15 and are copied here verbatim on 2026-09-18; 23 onward are the 2026-09-18 extension.

1. The board is the week view: the "Woche" tab position renders it for every role, labelled "Plantafel" for `admin` and `buero` and "Woche" for `employee`, whose board has exactly their own row.
2. Rows are active employees, grouped by P1-09 team with a collapsible team header, ordered by team then name; people without a login appear (they can be planned). An "Ohne Zuweisung" row at the top holds occurrences without assignees.
3. Columns are Berlin dates; the horizon is 1, 2, 4 or 6 weeks, chosen in the header and remembered per user; navigation by week, "Heute" scrolls the current date into view and marks it; weekends are visible but shaded; holidays and closure days shade the whole column with their label.
4. A card is one P1-11 occurrence: timed visits show start time and duration, all-day and multi-day occurrences render as one bar across their dates; cards show title, customer, place, job number and the P1-12 dispatch state (nicht gesendet, gesendet, bestätigt, Rückfrage, nicht möglich) as a chip; skipped and cancelled occurrences render muted and read-only; series occurrences carry the series mark.
5. Absences render in the row: approved vacation as "Urlaub", pending as dashed "angefragt", sickness as "Abwesend", spanning their dates; a person's row shows their schedule's non-working days shaded from P1-04.
6. Each person-day cell shows its capacity state from the P1-11 assessment for the visible window: free, partially planned, full, overbooked, with the planned and available minutes on hover and in a keyboard-reachable tooltip; the read is one window-level capacity call beside the existing window GET, never per cell.
7. Drag-and-drop: a card dragged to another row reassigns (same rules and confirmation dialogs as the day view: qualification, capacity, dispatch supersession); dragged to another column moves the date; the horizontal edge of an all-day bar extends or shortens it; dragged onto the Parkplatz parks it; a Parkplatz card dropped on a cell unparks it to that person and date. 8. Keyboard and form alternative for every drag: a card is focusable; Enter opens its edit dialog where date and assignees change; the existing planning dialogs are the form path.
9. Multi-assign: an occurrence with several assignees shows one card per assignee row, linked visually on hover; a drop on another row of a linked card adds or moves that assignment as the day view does today.
10. Filters: team, employee, job, customer, dispatch state and "nur Konflikte"; the member filter of the calendar applies; a free-text search matches title, customer and job number.
11. Freshness: the board reads through the range owner; another session's move, park, dispatch or absence change appears within `LIVE_TARGET_MS`; the retained-grid, stale and error states of the window contract apply unchanged.
12. Performance: opening the board for a six-week window of the typical profile is a registered measured scenario with a reviewed budget; the window read stays one GET plus one capacity GET.
13. Readiness chips: material "nicht reserviert" and tools "nicht bewertet" on every card, from the same readiness function the Einsätze panel uses, so the Wave 3 slices only change the values.
14. Employees never see the board; their occurrence visibility and acknowledgement flows are unchanged; organization isolation is proven for the capacity read.
15. No new table, enum or Realtime subscription; no message, reservation or order is caused by a board action.
16. Notes: an office user can add a note to a day or to a person-day from the board in two clicks; it renders as a distinct card (no capacity effect, no dispatch) and is an all-day `internal` occurrence of kind `Sonstiges` unless the owner chooses a lighter note kind (decision 14).
17. A read-only mode ("Nur ansehen") switch in the board header disables every drag until switched back; it is per user and remembered, so a second monitor in the office can show the board without accidental moves.
18. Keyboard: `t` today, `j` / `k` next and previous window, `z` undo of the last board move while its Undo banner is visible; the shortcuts are listed in a `?` overlay and never fire inside an input or dialog.
19. Layout: the grid scrolls sideways inside its own region with a visible edge (the existing canon exception), the name column stays sticky, and the board is usable on a tablet in landscape; on a phone width the employee's own row renders as a list, never a cropped grid.
20. Persistence: horizon, filters, search, the read-only switch and the actual-time toggle are remembered per user and organization; scroll position and the selected card survive a save, a Realtime update and a manual refresh.
21. Actual time: the "Ist-Zeiten anzeigen" toggle renders recorded time blocks and provisional correction badges in the row from the shared projection, defaulting on for the one-week horizon and off otherwise.
22. Drops on dispatched or committed occurrences show the existing supersession and commitment-mismatch dialogs before applying; a drop keeps the occurrence's duration and time of day; a series occurrence asks "nur dieser" or "diese und zukünftige" as the day view does.

25. Text contrast: every card, bar, chip and axis label meets 4.5:1 against its fill in both themes; no calendar text below 11 px; the absence and holiday bars are legible in the light theme.
26.
