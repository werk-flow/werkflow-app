Which error message placement do you choose?
A toast notification, a modal in the center of the screen,
or inline.
This is part seven of my series of building for good UX.
So let's break it down.
A toast is a message that pops up
at the top or the bottom of your screen,
and then it auto dismisses in a couple of seconds.
Don't use toast for an important error.
Think, if the user looks away and they miss this,
are they gonna be okay?
It's perfect for messages like couldn't connect, retrying.
A modal takes over the center of the screen
and blocks everything until the user responds.
And for this reason, use this sparingly.
Only when the user can't continue
without addressing the issue.
For example, a payment fails,
so modal pops up saying your payment did not process,
and it gives you an action,
which is a button to press to update your payment.
Or permissions error,
like you don't have access to this project,
with a button to request access.
If you're gonna block the user,
you have to give them a way forward.
Inline errors show up right next to the thing
that went wrong.
And as a general rule,
the closer your error is to your issue, the better.
So these are the ones
that you'll probably use most often.
For a form showing a red border
for a field that needs correction,
right next to a button that they just clicked.
If the user clicks save and it fails,
it should say try again right next to it.
The user's eyes are already there.
